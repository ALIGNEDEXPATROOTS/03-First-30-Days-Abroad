The First 30 Days Abroad

Your week-by-week landing guide. Tap to check tasks off as you go.

0% complete
You landed. It's loud, disorienting, and nothing looks the way it did in photos. This is normal. Your job this week is not to figure everything out — it's just to get safe, rested, and oriented.
Urgent — do first
✓
Urgent
Get a working SIM or activate your eSIM
Having data on arrival is non-negotiable. Find a local SIM vendor at the airport or activate Airalo before landing. Navigation, translation, and finding help all depend on this.
✓
Urgent
Confirm your accommodation is ready
Message your host or landlord before you board your flight. Have a backup plan in case something falls through — know the name of a nearby hotel.
✓
Urgent
Locate cash or activate your travel card
ATMs at airports often charge high fees. Have your Wise or Revolut card ready as backup. Taxis, deposits, and small shops often need cash in the first 48 hours.
Settling in
✓
Important
Walk your neighborhood — no agenda
Within 24 hours, walk a 15-minute circle around where you're staying. Find the nearest pharmacy, supermarket, café, and public transport. This one walk does more for your sense of safety than anything else.
✓
Wellbeing
Let the people who care about you know you arrived
A quick message prevents worry cascades. You don't need to be excited or chatty — just "I'm here, I'm okay" goes a long way.
✓
Wellbeing
Sleep, eat, and do nothing useful on Day 1
Resist the urge to optimize your first day. Your nervous system needs to register where it is. Eat something local, rest properly, and let the strangeness just exist around you.
The honeymoon phase and the panic phase sometimes arrive in the same afternoon. You're figuring out how to be a person in a new place. Focus on building the small routines that make a place feel less foreign.
Practical setup
✓
Urgent
Register your address if required by law
Many countries require foreign nationals to register within a specific window. This step unlocks your national ID number, banking, and other services. Don't skip it.
✓
Important
Open a local bank account or confirm your international card works here
Some countries require a local bank account for rent, utilities, or visa compliance. Research whether you need one and what documents are required.
✓
Important
Find a reliable regular grocery store
Knowing where to buy food cheaply and reliably in your area is one of the fastest ways to build a weekly rhythm — and rhythm is what turns "surviving" into "living."
Connections
✓
Wellbeing
Find one expat community or local event to attend
Meetup.com, Internations, Facebook expat groups for your city, or a local language exchange event. You just need one real conversation this week.
✓
Wellbeing
Find a café or spot you actually like
Your third place — not home, not work, but yours. Somewhere you can sit with a coffee and feel like you belong a little.
Week 2 is often when the emotional dip hits. The novelty wears off, the practical annoyances pile up, and you miss things you didn't expect to miss. This is not failure. This is settling in. Keep going.
Admin progress
✓
Important
Sort your longer-term housing plan
If you're in short-term accommodation, start seriously looking at longer-term options. Join local Facebook housing groups. Be realistic about what you can afford once you factor in utility deposits.
✓
Important
Research your nearest health clinic or doctor
Before you need it — not when you need it. Know where the nearest walk-in clinic is, how health insurance claims work abroad, and what basic medications are called locally.
✓
Important
Set up essential utilities if in longer-term housing
Internet is often the hardest and slowest to set up. Prioritise this. Electricity and water are usually tied to the landlord initially — confirm arrangements in writing.
Emotional check-in
✓
Wellbeing
Write down 3 things that have gone well so far
Not for social media. Just for yourself. The brain is wired to notice threats, not wins. Actively naming what's working builds real resilience.
By week 3, you know more than you realise. You have routes, rhythms, a few faces you recognise. Things are starting to make sense in a background sort of way. Keep building on that.
Building stability
✓
Important
Build a weekly rhythm that works for you
Anchor points in the week create a sense of home faster than anything else. A regular market, gym class, café morning, or language lesson — it just needs to be yours.
✓
Important
Learn 20 essential phrases in the local language
You don't need to be fluent. But "please", "thank you", "where is", "how much", "I don't understand" transforms how locals respond to you. Duolingo works. So does asking shopkeepers to correct you.
✓
Wellbeing
Stay in touch with one person from your old life each week
One check-in call per week with someone you love back home keeps your roots alive. Don't wait until you're lonely to reach out — stay connected before the loneliness sets in.
One month. That's real. Take a breath and look at how far you've come — not just logistically, but as a person who made a hard thing happen.
Reflection
✓
Wellbeing
Do a 30-day review — honestly
Ask yourself: What was harder than expected? What was easier? What do I wish I'd done differently? What's actually great here? Honest reflection now will shape how you handle month 2, 3, and beyond.
✓
Important
Review your visa timeline and upcoming requirements
Now that the chaos of landing has settled, double-check where you stand on your visa. Calendar everything now before months blur together.
✓
Wellbeing
Decide what you want your life here to actually look like
Month 1 was survival mode. Month 2 can be intentional. Set one meaningful intention for the next 30 days — not a task list, just a direction.
